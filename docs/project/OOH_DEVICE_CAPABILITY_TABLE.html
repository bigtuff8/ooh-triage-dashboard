OOH Triage Dashboard - Device Capability Table

One row per type of device the live OOH Triage Dashboard recognises: what a call handler can see and do for it today. Device types and models, not individual installed devices.

As of dashboard v1.4.1 (live, 15 October 2026). Ticket OOHDASH-131.

How to read this

  • The dashboard does not have a "device list" screen. Handlers see device information inside the guided flows (Heating, External lighting, and so on), in the "What Lighthouse controls at this site" panel, and in the red "Some Lighthouse equipment at this site is not responding" banner.
  • The two ThingsBoard columns go beyond the dashboard. They say what could be controlled, and what schedules could be seen, from ThingsBoard itself, whether or not the dashboard uses it. "Tested" means there is a dated record of a change actually landing on that type of device. "Theory" means there is only a reason to think it could work. "Direct" means a change made straight on the device record in ThingsBoard. "Via vendor bridge" means a change passed through the vendor's command gate and integration bridge to the maker's cloud. Full evidence, with device names, dates and sources, is in OOHDASH-131_THINGSBOARD_LAYER_EVIDENCE.md (read-only check of live ThingsBoard, 15 October 2026).
  • "Not reporting" or "offline" means the device has sent no data recently: 3 hours for thermostats and switched circuits, 48 hours for everything else.
  • Every change a handler makes is checked against the confirmed site, and is only shown as done once the device itself reports the new value.
  • "None" means nothing is available. "(unconfirmed)" means the statement comes from research notes, not from the dashboard code, and could not be confirmed from the code.

What Spencer said vs what we can evidence

Each claim is listed as found in the sources, with the evidence status as of 15 October 2026.

  • "You control every device" with one of four commands written straight to the device (Spencer's contract, email 17 September)
    Not supported. That method was replaced on 10 October. When it was used in production, Salus changes stuck at "pending" and lighting commands were never confirmed. It never applied to boiler panels or PowerPause.
  • Round trip "under 1 second for switches, a few seconds for thermostats" (contract, 17 September)
    Switches: true on one test socket (10 and 12 October) and on four bench switches (17 September). Thermostats: never observed, in production or on the bench.
  • Four controllable device types: Tuya switches, Salus thermostats, Intesis air-con, with boilers deferred (asset control matrix, 17 September)
    Tuya: one production device proven. Salus: not proven. Intesis: target temperature only, on test units in June, under the old method. Boilers: in fact controlled directly by the IoT team, outside the bridge.
  • External lighting, extract fans, over-door heaters and kitchen switches marked "control proven" (matrix, 17 September)
    Proven only on four development-bench switches under the old method. No production lighting circuit, fan, heater or kitchen circuit has a confirmed command.
  • Salus iT700 accepts temperature changes; the iT500 also accepts hot-water boost (contract, 17 September)
    No confirmed command on either model. No iT500 has reported since 28 September.
  • "Prod Tuya control is live and proven" (Teams, 10 October)
    True for one test socket at the Brackley test site only. 5 of the 14 commands sent to it between 10 and 12 October were refused by the vendor side (SQ-03).
  • Salus timing "being measured now, minutes not seconds" (Teams, 10 October)
    No figure has been published since, and no Salus command has been seen going through the new route (SQ-05, SQ-06).
  • "Commands go through ThingsBoard, which is the path proven above" (Teams, 10 October)
    True for Tuya. Boiler panel and PowerPause control does not use that route.
  • "Control and schedule visibility over most devices" (as relayed by James, 15 October; no written source found)
    Control confirmed today: one Tuya test socket via the vendor bridge, and boiler panels directly. Schedules can be read for boiler panels and PowerPause, decoded for Owon switches, and partly for iT700 (the follow-programme or on-hold state only). They are unreliable for Tongou switches, coded and out of date for the iT500 and older Salus, and missing for air-con. "Most devices" is not supported for control. It is roughly true for schedules on pub heating and kitchen power.

Questions to put to Spencer to close the gaps

These are new questions. Where an existing question already covers a point, it is referenced, not repeated: SQ-01 (command route), SQ-03 (Tuya rejections), SQ-04 (stuck pending), SQ-05 (Salus proof), SQ-06 and SQ-08 (Salus timing), SQ-07 (iT500 dormancy and what the older Salus units are), SQ-10 (boost key), SQ-11 (lighting state key), SQ-12 (offline window), SQ-13 (schedule meaning and decoding).

  1. Which device types do you consider controllable from ThingsBoard today, and which have visible schedules? For each, please give one device name and date where you saw it work in production. Today our evidence is one Tuya test socket.
  2. Your IoT team already overrides boiler zones directly in ThingsBoard: older panels by the zone override setting (gk-2933 on 25 September, gk-6780 on 7 October), and newer panels by the override device command (gk-6261 on 28 September). Is this a supported route the dashboard may use? What do the values 1, 0 and -1 mean, how long does an override last, and what does the panel report to confirm it? This relates to LH-INT-BOILER-001; SQ-13 covers schedule fields only.
  3. Older (V1) boiler panels: schedules were rewritten on gk-6780 on 10 and 14 October, and on gk-2933 on 21 May, but the copy each panel reports is still the 12 April version. Do these panels apply schedule changes, and how can we confirm one took effect? The newer panel gk-6261 echoed its 13 October change within a minute.
  4. PowerPause: is adding a dated entry to the circuit's override list the supported way to switch a circuit remotely? Does the circuit echo it, and has it been used in production? PowerPause does not go through the command gate.
  5. Many Tuya switches carry a stored timetable written by a separate tool between November 2025 and February 2026. Which tool writes it, is it kept in step with the device, and which source should we trust? For example, gk-6815-externallighting-1 stores off at 00:30, but the light went off at 23:00 on 13 and 14 October. SQ-13(b) asks only for the decoding rules.
  6. Can fridge, freezer and cellar devices reach the command gate at all? Could the gate refuse switching on refrigeration (for example the two-socket coolers at Brackley) as a second safety layer behind the dashboard's block?
  7. The dashboard's ThingsBoard login is shared with the remote-access sync job, which writes about every 5 minutes, so dashboard commands are hard to find in the audit log. Can the dashboard have its own login? SEC-1 covers the gate, not this.
  8. The only production Tuya proof is a test socket. Can the J8 test also cover one real external-lighting circuit, including a unit like gk-6237 that reports its state differently, and one real extractor fan? Or can you share a production run on those? SQ-11 covers the reading, not whether commands are accepted.
  9. SQ-13 says no schedule data exists in ThingsBoard for the iT500. But iT500 and older Salus units do hold a coded programme copy, last written 2 June. What is it, will it refresh when polling resumes, and can it be decoded? Please treat this as a correction to SQ-13(d).
  10. Intesis: the only success records are from test units on 12 June, under the old method. Has any Intesis temperature or mode command been accepted through the new route since 10 October? Separately, can the 246 Modbus air-con units be controlled at all? SQ-05 covers Salus only.
  11. The dashboard offers "hold until the next schedule slot" on Salus thermostats. Through the bridge, does a temperature change become a temporary hold that ends at the next programme step, or a permanent hold? Can the bridge send "resume programme"? gk-6261 shows as on hold today.
  12. Old commands from June to October are still sitting on devices (Salus gk-6261, gk-6832 and gk-1648; lighting gk-6237 and gk-6815; Intesis md-1244). Will they be cleared? Could one be re-sent by the bridge after a restart? SQ-04 covers stuck "pending", not this.

Key gaps worth a look

  1. Switched devices with unrecognised names (over-door heaters, immersion heaters, festoon lights, supply, loft or bar fans) are all grouped as "kitchen", so a handler cannot switch them, and they never appear in the Lighting or Fans flows.
  2. The "What Lighthouse controls at this site" panel can say "Controllable from here" for Kitchen equipment, Heating (any pub with a boiler panel) and Hot water, while the flows offer no control for those. A handler could tell a caller something can be changed when it cannot. Lighting and Extractor fans are also shown as "Controllable" whenever such a device exists, even when it is offline and the flow says it cannot be switched. The reverse also happens: at a site with an iT700 thermostat but no boiler panel, the panel shows Heating as "Monitored only", yet the Heating flow does offer temperature changes. A handler could tell a caller heating cannot be changed when it can.
  3. iT500 and older Salus thermostats are labelled as not controllable, but nothing enforces it. This is a real exposure, currently latent (not happening today only because these units are offline), and it is not just a screen issue:
    • The server does not check whether a device is marked not controllable before it sends a command, so a request made outside the screens would also be carried out.
    • If any iT500 or older Salus thermostat in an area is reporting, the Heating flow offers Warmer, Cooler and Turn it off for it.
    • The same applies to devices treated as switches from their name alone, without a switch reading: the flows offer Turn ON or OFF and the server accepts it.
    • Raised as a Bug - see linked ticket OOHDASH-132.
  4. Lighting and Extractor fans show only the first circuit at a site. A site with several lighting or fan circuits shows, and can switch, just one.
  5. No temperature or status readings are shown for fridges, air conditioning or the boiler panel, so a handler cannot check a caller's report against live data for those.
  6. The boiler control panel is not recognised as its own type, so the planned "pub boiler panel" reading and guidance never appears. It shows as an unknown device.
  7. Remote changes are still offered when the site hub is offline, as long as the device itself shows online.
  8. Older Salus units named without a bracketed site code are still missing (OOHDASH-129).

Cost and benefit of acting on these gaps

These are rough estimates for James's review and have not been sized by the build team.

  • Gaps 1, 2 and 4 are the cheapest and most valuable: each is a small change to how devices are grouped or labelled, about 0.5 to 2 days each including tests. The benefit is that handlers stop being told something is controllable when it is not, and they gain switching on fans and lights they cannot reach today.
  • Gap 3 is about half a day to a day: one server check that refuses commands to devices marked as not controllable, plus hiding the controls on screen. The benefit today is low because the units are offline, but it closes a real exposure before any unit starts reporting again.
  • Gaps 5 and 6 are larger, about 3 to 10 days each, because the readings need to be designed, worded and tested. The benefit is better triage of fridge, air-con and pub-heating calls. They need James to decide whether they are worth doing.
  • Gap 7 is a design decision rather than a fix. The risk of leaving it is low for cloud-connected switches, and higher for Salus thermostats.
